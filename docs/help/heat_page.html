Input (pre-processing)

Heat transport

The page holds the Heat Transport Parameters and Heat Transport Boundary Conditions of HYDRUS-1D. The equations are in heat transport.

Parameters per material

ParameterDescription
θnvolume fraction of the solid phase [–] (≈ 1 − porosity)
θovolume fraction of organic matter [–]
λLlongitudinal thermal dispersivity [L]
b1, b2, b3coefficients of the thermal conductivity λ0(θ) = b1 + b2 θ + b3 θ0.5 Chung and Horton (1987)
Cn, Co, Cwvolumetric heat capacities of the solid phase, organic matter and water de Vries (1963)

The thermal parameters are in the project's units with the mass in kg (units). The buttons enter typical values converted to the project's units:

ButtonValues
b1, b2, b3 for sand / loam / clayChung and Horton (1987): sand 0.228, −2.406, 4.909; loam 0.243, 0.393, 1.534; clay −0.197, −0.962, 2.521 W/m/K
Default heat capacitiesCn = 1.92, Co = 2.51, Cw = 4.18 MJ/m³/K

Campbell (1985) thermal conductivity uses the function of Campbell (1985) instead; b1, b2 and b3 then hold the volume fractions of quartz, other minerals and clay.

Boundary conditions

InputDescription
Upper boundarytemperature (first type) or heat flux (third type: the heat carried by the entering water)
Lower boundarytemperature, heat flux or zero gradient
Upper / lower temperature[°C]; overridden by the Ttop / Tbot columns of the time-variable data
Amplitude, periodsinusoidal daily variation of the surface temperature, T(t) = Ttop + A sin(2π t / p − 7π/12) with the amplitude A and the period p; the maximum is reached at 13:00 for p = 1 d

With the surface energy balance the surface temperature follows from the energy balance instead.

The initial temperatures are set on the page Soil profile.

  • de Vries, D. A. (1963). Thermal properties of soils. In: W. R. van Wijk (ed.), Physics of Plant Environment, North-Holland, Amsterdam, pp. 210–235.
