Theory and equations

Dual-porosity models

Dual-porosity models assume that water flows only in one part of the pore space (inter-aggregate pores, fractures, the mobile region), while water in the other part (intra-aggregate pores, the rock matrix, the immobile region) does not move but exchanges water and solutes with the mobile region Šimůnek et al. (2003); Šimůnek and van Genuchten (2008). They describe non-equilibrium (preferential) flow in structured soils.

Governing equations

∂θmo/∂t = ∂/∂x [ K(h) (∂h/∂x + cos α) ] − Smo − Γw
∂θim/∂t = −Sim + Γw
θ = θmo + θim

θmo and θim are the water contents of the mobile and immobile regions, Smo and Sim their sink terms and Γw the transfer rate of water from the mobile to the immobile region [1/T]. The hydraulic functions of the mobile region are van Genuchten–Mualem functions; the immobile region has its own retention parameters θr,im, θs,im (and αim, nim).

Mass transfer driven by the water content

The transfer rate is proportional to the difference of the effective saturations of the two regions Šimůnek et al. (2001); Šimůnek et al. (2003):

Γw = ω [Semo − Seim]

with the first-order rate coefficient ω [1/T]. The transfer within a time step is limited so that the immobile region does not exceed saturation or fall below its residual water content.

Mass transfer driven by the pressure head

The transfer is driven by the difference of the pressure heads of the two regions, with a conductivity of the interface evaluated with the van Genuchten parameters of the immobile region (αim, nim; ω takes the role of the saturated conductivity of the interface):

Γw = ½ [Ka(hmo) + Ka(him)] (hmo − him)

where him is the head of the immobile region at its current water content.

Durner's model

The model of Durner (1994) also has two pore regions, but in equilibrium: one Richards equation with a bimodal retention function (Durner).

Solute transport

With a dual-porosity flow model, solute transport uses the mobile–immobile formulation with an exchange proportional to the concentration difference plus the solute carried by the water transfer (non-equilibrium transport).

See also: dual-permeability model
