Solute transport: reactions

The reaction parameters per solute and material, the second part of the Solute Transport Parameters dialog of HYDRUS-1D (Solute Reaction Parameters). The equations are in solute transport.

Parameters

ParameterHYDRUSDescription
KsKdadsorption coefficient [L³/M] of the isotherm s = Ks cβ/(1 + η cβ); for linear sorption the distribution coefficient Kd
ηNuLangmuir coefficient [L³/M]; 0 = Freundlich or linear
βBetaFreundlich exponent [–]; 1 = linear
Henry kHHenryHenry's constant, g = kH c [–] (volatile solutes) Jury et al. (1983)
μw, μs, μgSinkL1, SinkS1, SinkG1first-order decay constants in the liquid, solid and gas phases [1/T]
γw, γs, γgSinkL1', SinkS1', SinkG1'first-order production of this solute from the previous one (decay chain) [1/T]
μ0w, μ0s, μ0gSinkL0, SinkS0, SinkG0zero-order production in the three phases [M/L³/T, 1/T] (negative values: zero-order decay)
α (rate)Alfafirst-order rate of the non-equilibrium exchange: kinetic sorption sites (f < 1) or mobile–immobile water (θim > 0) [1/T]
αs–dual permeability: solute transfer coefficient between the fractures and the matrix [1/T]

With dual non-equilibrium the columns μg and γg are reused: μg is the fraction fem of equilibrium sites in the mobile zone and γg the rate ωS of the kinetic sites, as in HYDRUS-1D. With the colloid model the columns are reused for the attachment parameters (colloids).

Hints

  • For a conservative tracer leave all reaction parameters at 0 (Ks = 0, β = 1).
  • The retardation factor of linear sorption is R = 1 + ρ Kd / θ.
  • The half-life of first-order decay is t1/2 = ln 2 / μ.
