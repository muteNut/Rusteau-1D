Theory and equations

Atmospheric inputs: partitioning, daily variation, interception, snow

Partitioning of the potential evapotranspiration

When only the potential evapotranspiration ETp is known, it is divided into potential transpiration Tp and potential evaporation Ep with the leaf area index LAI Ritchie (1972):

SCF = 1 − exp(−k · LAI)
Tp = ETp · SCF, Ep = ETp (1 − SCF)

SCF is the soil cover fraction and k the extinction coefficient of the radiation in the canopy (HYDRUS default 0.463). The same partitioning is used with meteorological data.

Daily variation of evaporation and transpiration

Daily values of evaporation and transpiration can be distributed over the day with the functions of Fayer (2000), as in HYDRUS: during the night (t < 0.264 d and t > 0.736 d) the rate is 24 % of the daily mean, during the day it follows a sine wave:

Tp(t) = 2.75 Tp sin(2π t − π/2) for 0.264 d ≤ t ≤ 0.736 d; Tp(t) = 0.24 Tp otherwise

with t the time of the day in days; the integral over the day equals the daily rate. The same applies to the evaporation.

Canopy interception

Part of the precipitation is intercepted by the leaves and evaporates. The interception follows von Hoyningen-Hüne (1983) and Braden (1985):

I = a · LAI [ 1 − 1 / (1 + SCF · P / (a · LAI)) ]

with the precipitation P, the interception constant a [L] (the interception capacity is a·LAI) and the soil cover fraction SCF. The intercepted water evaporates first and reduces the potential transpiration; the rest reaches the soil.

Snow

With snow accumulation, precipitation falls as snow at air temperatures below −2 °C, as rain above +2 °C, and as a mixture in between (the snow fraction decreases linearly from 1 to 0 between −2 and +2 °C). Once a snow layer exists, all precipitation is added to it. The snow melts at air temperatures above 0 °C with a degree-day factor mf [L/T/°C]:

M = mf · Ta for Ta > 0 °C

(a temperature-index approach, see Hock (2003)); the HYDRUS default is 0.43 cm/d/°C. Evaporation is taken from the snow layer first. Solutes in the precipitation are stored in the snow and released with the melt water.

Sinusoidal precipitation

With sinusoidal precipitation, the precipitation of each record follows a sine wave over the record's interval instead of a constant rate, with the same total.
