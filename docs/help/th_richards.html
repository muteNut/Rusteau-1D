Theory and equations

Variably saturated water flow

One-dimensional uniform (equilibrium) water flow in a partially saturated rigid porous medium is described by a modified form of the Richards equation, assuming that the air phase plays an insignificant role in the liquid flow process and that water flow due to thermal gradients can be neglected Šimůnek et al. (2009):

∂θ/∂t = ∂/∂x [ K (∂h/∂x + cos α) ] − S
SymbolMeaning
hpressure head [L] (negative in unsaturated soil)
θvolumetric water content [L³/L³]
ttime [T]
xspatial coordinate [L], positive upward (the interface uses the depth below the surface, positive downward)
Ssink term [L³/L³/T], the root water uptake
αangle between the flow direction and the vertical axis (α = 0° for vertical flow, 90° for horizontal flow)
Kunsaturated hydraulic conductivity [L/T]

The unsaturated hydraulic conductivity is the product of the saturated conductivity and the relative conductivity, scaled locally (scaling):

K(h, x) = Ks(x) Kr(h, x)

The functions θ(h) and K(h) – the soil hydraulic properties – are given by one of the soil hydraulic models. The water capacity C(h) = dθ/dh appears when the equation is written in terms of h; the numerical solution uses the mass-conservative mixed form with θ and h Celia et al. (1990) (numerical solution).

Extensions

  • Hysteresis: θ(h) and K(h) depend on the wetting and drying history (hysteresis).
  • Non-equilibrium flow: dual-porosity and dual-permeability formulations with exchange terms between two regions (dual porosity, dual permeability), or a delayed approach of θ to equilibrium (further options).
  • Vapor flow and temperature: with vapor flow the equation includes the isothermal and thermal vapor fluxes and the thermal liquid flux Saito et al. (2006) (vapor flow); with temperature-dependent hydraulic properties h and K are scaled with the temperature (hydraulic models).
  • Centrifuge and density-dependent flow: the gravity term cos α is replaced (further options).

Boundary and initial conditions

The initial condition is the pressure head (or water content) of every node, set on the page Soil profile. The boundary conditions at the surface and the bottom are described in boundary conditions.
