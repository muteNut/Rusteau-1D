Theory and equations

Colloid, virus and bacteria transport

Colloids, viruses and bacteria are transported like solutes but are retained by kinetic attachment to the solid phase rather than by sorption. HYDRUS-1D (and Rusteau) uses two kinetic sites with attachment and detachment Schijven and Šimůnek (2002); Bradford et al. (2003):

∂(θ c)/∂t + ρ ∂se/∂t + ρ ∂s1/∂t + ρ ∂s2/∂t = ∂/∂x (θ D ∂c/∂x) − ∂(q c)/∂x − R
ρ ∂si/∂t = θ ψi katt,i c − kdet,i ρ si − (decay)

with the concentration c of the colloids in the liquid, the concentrations s1, s2 attached to the two sites, the attachment and detachment coefficients katt,i, kdet,i [1/T] and the dimensionless functions ψi that describe blocking, ripening or straining.

Blocking and straining functions

iPsiFunction
0no blocking, ψ = 1
1Langmuir blocking, ψ = 1 − s/smax
2ripening: attachment increases with the attached mass
3random sequential adsorption (RSA) Adamczyk et al. (1994); Johnson and Elimelech (1995)
4depth-dependent straining, ψ = ((dc + z − z0)/dc)−β Bradford et al. (2003)
5straining combined with Langmuir blocking

smax (SMax) is the maximum solid-phase concentration of a site and dc the diameter of the sand grains.

Attachment from filtration theory

With attachment rates from filtration theory, the attachment coefficient follows from clean-bed filtration theory Logan et al. (1995):

katt = 3(1 − n) / (2 dc) · η · α · v

with the porosity n, the collector (grain) diameter dc, the single-collector efficiency η Rajagopalan and Tien (1976), the sticking efficiency α (entered in the column of the attachment coefficient) and the pore-water velocity v. The particle diameter dp enters η.

Parameters

The parameters are entered per solute (colloid) and material on the page T / θ dependence, colloids: rKa1, rKd1, SMax1, rKa2, rKd2, SMax2 and the blocking types or the diameters Dc, Dp.
