Solute transport: general

The page corresponds to the General Solute Transport Information dialog of HYDRUS-1D: the numerical scheme, the transport model and the names of the solutes. The equations are in solute transport.

Numerical scheme

InputDescription
Time weighting ε0 explicit, 0.5 Crank–Nicolson (default, second order), 1 fully implicit
Max. iterations (non-linear)iterations per time step with non-linear sorption
Absolute / relative concentration toleranceconvergence of the iteration with non-linear sorption
Stability criterion Pe_critPe·Cr ≤ Pe_crit (default 2) Perrochet and Berod (1993)
Upstream weightingupstream-weighted finite elements Christie et al. (1976) instead of Galerkin; damps oscillations at sharp fronts
Add artificial dispersionwith Galerkin elements, artificial dispersion is added where the stability criterion is not met

See numerical solution and discretization.

Transport model

InputDescription
Tortuosity factormolecular diffusion reduced by the tortuosity of the liquid and gas phases, after Millington and Quirk (1961) or Moldrup and Olesen Moldrup et al. (1997); Moldrup et al. (2000)
Non-equilibrium phase initially in equilibriumthe initial kinetically sorbed or immobile concentrations are in equilibrium with the liquid
Initial conditions as total solute massthe initial concentrations of the profile are total masses per volume of soil
Reported concentrationresident (volume-averaged) or flux-averaged concentrations
Dual non-equilibriummobile–immobile water plus kinetic sorption sites in the mobile zone (needs θim > 0) Šimůnek and van Genuchten (2008)
Decay products … to the non-equilibrium phasethe daughter products of the kinetically sorbed mass enter the kinetic sites of the next solute (lNEquil)
Colloid / virus / bacteria transporttwo kinetic attachment–detachment sites with blocking and straining Schijven and Šimůnek (2002); Bradford et al. (2003) (colloids); optionally with attachment from filtration theory
Temperature dependenceArrhenius dependence of the transport and reaction parameters Stumm and Morgan (1981)
Water content dependent reaction ratesafter Walker (1974) or trapezoidal functions per reaction (theory)

Solutes

The names of the solutes (as many as set on Processes & units). Solutes form a decay chain: solute k is produced from solute k−1 by the first-order coefficients γ on the reaction page.
