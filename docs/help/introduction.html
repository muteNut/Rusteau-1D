Introduction

Introduction to Rusteau-1D

Rusteau-1D is a program for simulating the one-dimensional movement of water, heat and multiple solutes in variably saturated porous media, such as the unsaturated (vadose) zone of a soil profile, laboratory columns or lysimeters. It solves the same equations with the same numerical methods as HYDRUS-1D Šimůnek et al. (2009); Šimůnek et al. (2008); Šimůnek et al. (2016): the numerical engine is a port of the HYDRUS-1D Fortran source (version 4.08) to Rust, and where HYDRUS-1D 4.17 behaves differently from that source, Rusteau follows 4.17 Šimůnek et al. (2013).

What the program computes

  • Variably saturated water flow with the Richards equation, including a sink term for root water uptake (theory). The soil hydraulic properties can be described by the models of van Genuchten–Mualem van Genuchten (1980); Mualem (1976), modified van Genuchten Vogel and Císlerová (1988), Brooks and Corey (1964), Kosugi (1996) and Durner (1994), or by look-up tables (hydraulic models).
  • Hysteresis in the retention curve and the hydraulic conductivity Kool and Parker (1987), or without pumping Lenhard et al. (1991) (hysteresis).
  • Non-equilibrium (preferential) flow with dual-porosity and dual-permeability models Šimůnek et al. (2003); Gerke and van Genuchten (1993a) (dual porosity, dual permeability).
  • Root water and solute uptake with water and osmotic stress, compensation and active uptake Feddes et al. (1978); van Genuchten (1987); Šimůnek and Hopmans (2009) (root uptake).
  • Atmospheric boundary conditions from precipitation and potential evapotranspiration, or from meteorological data with the Penman–Monteith or Hargreaves equations Allen et al. (1998); Hargreaves and Samani (1985), snow, interception and the surface energy balance Saito et al. (2006) (atmosphere, energy balance).
  • Heat transport by conduction and convection, coupled with water vapor flow Saito et al. (2006) (heat, vapor).
  • Solute transport of several solutes with linear or non-linear sorption, decay chains, gas-phase diffusion, physical and chemical non-equilibrium, and colloid / virus transport Šimůnek and van Genuchten (2008); Schijven and Šimůnek (2002) (solute transport).
  • Parameter estimation (inverse problem) by the Marquardt–Levenberg method from measured data Marquardt (1963); Šimůnek and Hopmans (2002) (theory).
  • Pedotransfer functions: soil hydraulic parameters from the texture with Rosetta Schaap et al. (2001); Zhang and Schaap (2017) (Rosetta).

Verification

All 23 examples of the HYDRUS-1D distribution and its 12 parameter-estimation examples, recalculated with HYDRUS 4.17, are part of the test suite of the program. Most examples agree with HYDRUS within the print precision of the output files; the remaining differences are understood and documented (relation to HYDRUS-1D). End-of-run water and solute totals agree within 0.07 %.

The interface

The interface leads through a project in the order of the input: processes and units, soil, boundary conditions, uptake and transport, run (workflow). Projects are stored as Rusteau projects (.h1dr) and can be read from and written to HYDRUS-1D 4.x project folders, so a project made in Rusteau opens in HYDRUS-1D (projects). Every input has a tooltip with its meaning, unit and typical values; F1 opens the help of the current page.

Not included

HP1 (the coupling of HYDRUS with the geochemical code PHREEQC) and the UNSATCHEM major-ion chemistry module are separate codes beside the HYDRUS-1D core and are not part of Rusteau-1D. The carbon dioxide transport of UNSATCHEM is not included either.
