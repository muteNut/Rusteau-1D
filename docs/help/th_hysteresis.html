Theory and equations

Hysteresis

The relation between water content and pressure head depends on whether the soil is wetting or drying. The water content on a drying branch is higher than on a wetting branch at the same head (ink-bottle effect, contact angle, entrapped air).

Kool and Parker model

HYDRUS incorporates hysteresis with the empirical model of Scott et al. (1983), as modified by Kool and Parker (1987) to account for air entrapment. The main drying curve has the parameters θr, θs, α, n (and θm); the main wetting curve has the same θr and n and its own θsw, αw (often αw ≈ 2α). With hysteresis in the conductivity, the main wetting curve also has Ksw.

Scanning curves start at a reversal point (hΔ, θΔ), where the direction of the change of the water content changes. They are scaled from the main curves so that they pass through the reversal point:

  • A drying scanning curve is the main drying curve scaled towards θr:
θ(h) = θr + ad [θd(h) − θr], ad = (θΔ − θr) / (θd(hΔ) − θr)
  • A wetting scanning curve is the main wetting curve scaled so that it passes through the reversal point and reaches, at saturation, the water content θs that remains after air entrapment:
θ(h) = θr + aw [θw(h) − θr], θr = θs − aw (θsw − θr)
θs = θs − (θs − θΔ) / [1 + R (θs − θΔ)], R = 1/(θs − θsw) − 1/(θs − θr)

The trapped-air relation for θs follows Land (1968), as in Kool and Parker (1987): wetting from a dry state ends at θsw, wetting from saturation ends at θs. With hysteresis in the conductivity, the scanning curves of K are scaled in the same way between K at the reversal point and Ks.

A node changes its branch when its water content changes in the opposite direction by more than the water content tolerance. The initial condition lies on the main drying or the main wetting curve (Initially drying / wetting curve), or on the scanning curves of a previous run (restart).

The pumping effect and the model of Lenhard et al.

While simple to implement, the Kool–Parker model can suffer from a pumping effect: under repeated small wetting and drying cycles the hysteresis loops can drift to physically unrealistic parts of the retention function. The model of Lenhard et al. (1991) and Lenhard and Parker (1992) eliminates pumping by keeping track of the historical reversal points: a scanning curve that returns to an earlier reversal point continues on the curve of that earlier reversal (closed scanning loops). Hysteresis in the retention curve (no pumping, Lenhard et al.) selects it.

Parameters

The hysteresis parameters are entered on the page Materials & parameters: θm (drying), θs, α and Ks of the wetting branch. For the parameter estimation the constraints θm = θs, αw = 2α and Ksw = Ks are available.
