Theory and equations

Scaling of the hydraulic properties

Spatial variability of the soil hydraulic properties within a material is described by a set of linear scaling transformations that relate the individual functions θ(h) and K(h) of a node to reference functions θ(h) and K(h) of its material Vogel et al. (1991); Šimůnek et al. (2009):

θ(h) = θr + αθ [θ(h) − θr]
K(h) = αK K(h)
h = αh h

with the three independent scaling factors αθ, αK and αh of the node (the quantities Scaling αθ, αK, αh of the soil profile). The approach is a generalization of the similar-media scaling of Miller and Miller (1956), in which αh and αK follow from a single characteristic length λ (αh = 1/λ, αK = λ², αθ = 1).

All factors equal to 1 mean no scaling. With hysteresis the scaling applies to both branches. Scaling factors are used, for example, to describe stochastic fields of hydraulic properties or gradual changes within a layer.
