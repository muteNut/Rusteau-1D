Theory and equations

Soil hydraulic property models

The unsaturated soil hydraulic properties θ(h) and K(h) are described with closed-form equations Šimůnek et al. (2009). In all models the effective saturation is

Se = (θ − θr) / (θs − θr)

with the residual and saturated water contents θr and θs.

van Genuchten – Mualem

van Genuchten (1980) used the statistical pore-size distribution model of Mualem (1976) to obtain a predictive equation for the unsaturated conductivity from the retention function:

θ(h) = θr + (θs − θr) / [1 + |αh|n]m for h < 0; θ(h) = θs for h ≥ 0
K(h) = Ks Sel [1 − (1 − Se1/m)m]2, m = 1 − 1/n, n > 1

α [1/L], n and l are empirical parameters; l is the pore-connectivity parameter, estimated as 0.5 by Mualem (1976) (the parameter l).

van Genuchten with an air-entry value of −2 cm

For fine-textured soils with n close to 1 the van Genuchten–Mualem conductivity drops steeply just below saturation, which makes the numerical solution unstable and the predictions poor. Vogel et al. (2001) and Schaap and van Genuchten (2006) introduced a small minimum capillary height hs = −2 cm: the retention curve is the van Genuchten function with the fictitious saturated water content θm chosen so that θ(hs) = θs,

θm = θr + (θs − θr) (1 + |α hs|n)m

and the conductivity follows the modified model below with θa = θr, θk = θs, Kk = Ks.

Modified van Genuchten (Vogel and Císlerová)

Vogel and Císlerová (1988) added flexibility near saturation with a non-zero air-entry value hs:

θ(h) = θa + (θm − θa) / (1 + |αh|n)m for h < hs; θ(h) = θs for h ≥ hs
K(h) = Ks Kr(h) for h ≤ hk; K(h) = Kk + (h − hk)(Ks − Kk)/(hs − hk) for hk < h < hs; K(h) = Ks for h ≥ hs
Kr = (Kk/Ks) (Se/Sek)l [ (F(θr) − F(θ)) / (F(θr) − F(θk)) ]2
F(θ) = [1 − ((θ − θa)/(θm − θa))1/m]m, Sek = (θk − θr)/(θs − θr)

θm ≥ θs and θa ≤ θr are fictitious extrapolations of the retention curve; Kk is a measured conductivity at the water content θk (hk = h(θk)). With θa = θr, θm = θk = θs and Kk = Ks the original van Genuchten–Mualem model results.

Brooks and Corey

Se = |αh|−n for h < −1/α; Se = 1 for h ≥ −1/α
K = Ks Se2/n + l + 2

Brooks and Corey (1964); here n is the pore-size distribution index (λ) and 1/α the air-entry (bubbling) pressure head.

Kosugi (log-normal)

Kosugi (1996) assumed a log-normal pore-size distribution; with Mualem's model:

Se = ½ erfc[ ln(h/h0) / (√2 σ) ] for h < 0
K = Ks Sel { ½ erfc[ ln(h/h0) / (√2 σ) + σ/√2 ] }2

In the parameter table α holds h0 (the median pressure head) and n holds σ (the standard deviation of ln |h|), as in HYDRUS.

Durner (dual porosity)

Durner (1994) divided the pore space into two overlapping regions, each with a van Genuchten–Mualem function; their linear superposition describes multimodal pore systems:

Se = w1 [1 + (α1 h)n1]−m1 + w2 [1 + (α2 h)n2]−m2
K(Se) = Ks (w1 Se1 + w2 Se2)l ( w1 α1 [1 − (1 − Se11/m1)m1] + w2 α2 [1 − (1 − Se21/m2)m2] )2 / (w1 α1 + w2 α2)2

with the weights w1 = 1 − w2 and mi = 1 − 1/ni.

Look-up tables

Instead of a model, θ(h), K(h) and C(h) can be given as tables (file Mater.in of HYDRUS-1D); values between the tabulated heads are interpolated.

Temperature dependence

With temperature-dependent hydraulic properties, the pressure head is scaled with the surface tension σ and the conductivity with the dynamic viscosity μ and the density ρ of water at the soil temperature T, relative to a reference temperature Philip and de Vries (1957); Constantz (1982):

h(T) = h(Tref) σ(T) / σ(Tref)
K(T) = K(Tref) [μ(Tref) ρ(T)] / [μ(T) ρ(Tref)]

The conductivity of water-saturated soil increases by about 2 % per °C, mostly through the viscosity.

Typical values

The soil catalog of Carsel and Parrish (1988) gives average parameters of the textural classes; Rosetta predicts them from the texture Schaap et al. (2001); Zhang and Schaap (2017).
