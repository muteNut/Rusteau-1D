Input (pre-processing)

Water flow boundary conditions

The page sets the boundary conditions of the water flow at the soil surface and at the bottom of the profile (the Water Flow Boundary Conditions dialog of HYDRUS-1D). The equations are in boundary conditions.

Upper boundary

ConditionDescription
Constant pressure headThe head of the top node keeps its initial value (set it on the page Soil profile).
Constant fluxA constant flux rTop [L/T]; negative = infiltration.
Atmospheric BC with surface layerPrecipitation and potential evaporation from the time-variable data; water that cannot infiltrate ponds at the surface up to hCritS and then runs off; evaporation is limited by the dry limit hCritA Neuman et al. (1974).
Atmospheric BC with surface runoffAs above without ponding: excess water runs off immediately.
Variable pressure headHead hTop of the time-variable data.
Variable fluxFlux Evap − Prec of the time-variable data.
Variable pressure head / fluxEach record decides with the code in the Evap column (positive: head, negative: flux).

hCritS is the maximum height of water at the surface before runoff (with the surface layer; 0 = no ponding). Constant potential transpiration is the potential transpiration with constant boundary conditions and root water uptake.

Triggered irrigation

With an atmospheric upper boundary, irrigation can be triggered by the pressure head at an observation node Šimůnek et al. (2016):

InputDescription
Observation nodenumber in the list of observation nodes (1 = first)
Pressure head triggering irrigationthreshold [L]; the irrigation is triggered when the head falls below it
Irrigation rate[L/T]
Irrigation duration[T]
Lag timetime between the trigger and the start [T]
BehaviourAs HYDRUS 4.17: the rate replaces the precipitation; the next atmospheric record ends the irrigation, and its end removes the precipitation until the following record. Full duration: the rate is added to the precipitation for the whole duration (Rusteau option, not stored in HYDRUS-1D projects).

The irrigation events are written to IRRIG.csv (output files).

Lower boundary

ConditionDescription
Constant pressure headThe head of the bottom node keeps its initial value (e.g. a groundwater table).
Constant fluxA constant flux rBot [L/T]; positive = upward (into the profile).
Variable pressure head / fluxhBot or rBot of the time-variable data.
Free drainageUnit hydraulic gradient: the water leaves with the conductivity of the bottom node; for deep water tables.
Deep drainageq = −Aqh exp(Bqh |h − GWL0L|) with the reference groundwater level GWL0L Hopmans and Stricker (1989).
Seepage faceNo flux while the bottom is unsaturated; drains at the head hSeep (usually 0) when saturated; lysimeters and columns with an open bottom.
Horizontal drainsDischarge to parallel drains or ditches after Hooghoudt (1940) and Ernst (1962), as implemented in SWAP van Dam et al. (1997).

Horizontal drains

InputDescription
Drain geometry / configuration1 homogeneous profile, drain on the impervious layer; 2 homogeneous, above the impervious layer; 3 layered, drain at the interface; 4 layered, drain in the bottom layer; 5 layered, drain in the top layer
Elevation of the drain bottomz coordinate [L] (negative)
Drain spacingdistance between the drains [L]
Entrance resistance[T]
Elevation of the impervious layer, wet perimeterconfigurations 2–5
Kh above / below the drain, Kv top / bottom, depth of the layer interface, geometry factorconductivities of the layers, depending on the configuration
