Theory and equations

Root solute uptake

Root solute (nutrient) uptake follows Šimůnek and Hopmans (2009) as the sum of a passive and an active part.

Passive uptake

Solutes taken up passively move with the water into the roots, up to a maximum concentration cmax (cRoot):

Pa(x, t) = S(x, t) min[c(x, t), cmax]

with the root water uptake S. cmax = 0 means no passive uptake; a large cmax means that the solute is taken up with the water at its full concentration.

Active uptake

The potential active uptake Ap is the part of the potential (demand) uptake rate that the passive uptake does not cover:

Ap(t) = max[ Spot ωw − ∫ Pa dx, 0 ]

where Spot is the potential solute uptake rate [M/L²/T] (with time-variable boundary conditions from the cBot column of the solute) and ωw the water stress index when reduced potential solute uptake due to reduced water uptake is on (otherwise 1). It is distributed over the root zone with Michaelis–Menten kinetics:

Aa(x, t) = b(x) Ap(t) (c − cmin) / (Km + c − cmin)

with the Michaelis–Menten constant Km and the minimum concentration cmin below which no active uptake occurs. As for water, the active uptake can be compensated: with the solute stress index ωs = ∫ Aa dx / Ap and the critical index ωs,c, the uptake is divided by max(ωs, ωs,c).

The total uptake Pa + Aa is a sink term of the solute transport equation of the solute. HYDRUS-1D 2009 allows active uptake of a single solute only; HYDRUS 4.17 and Rusteau let you choose the solute Šimůnek et al. (2016).
