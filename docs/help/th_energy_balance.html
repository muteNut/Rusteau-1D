Theory and equations

Surface energy balance

With the option Evaporation and heat flux from the surface energy balance (page Meteorological data) the evaporation is not prescribed but follows, together with the soil heat flux, from the energy balance at the soil surface Saito et al. (2006); van Bavel and Hillel (1976):

Rn − H − L E − G = 0
SymbolMeaning
Rnnet radiation [W/m²]
Hsensible heat flux [W/m²]
L Elatent heat flux [W/m²]: the evaporation E times the latent heat of vaporization L
Gsoil heat flux [W/m²], the upper boundary condition of the heat transport

It requires heat transport and is usually combined with vapor flow.

Net radiation

Rn = (1 − a) Rs + RL↓ − RL↑
RL↑ = εs σ Ts4, RL↓ = εa σ Ta4
  • the surface albedo a depends on the water content θ of the surface van Bavel and Hillel (1976): 0.25 for θ ≤ 0.1, 0.35 − θ for 0.1 < θ ≤ 0.25, 0.1 for θ > 0.25;
  • the soil emissivity εs = min(0.9 + 0.18 θ, 1) van Bavel and Hillel (1976);
  • the clear-sky emissivity of the atmosphere εac = 1.24 (ea/Ta)1/7 Brutsaert (1975), corrected for the cloud cover c: εa = (1 − 0.84 c) εac + 0.84 c Campbell and Norman (1998);
  • daily radiation is distributed over the day with the sine of the solar elevation.

Sensible and latent heat

H = Ca (Ts − Ta) / rh
E = (ρvs − ρva) / (rv + rs)

Ca is the volumetric heat capacity of the air, Ts and Ta the surface and air temperatures, ρvs the water vapor density at the soil surface (the saturated vapor density times the relative humidity of the soil air, from the pressure head and temperature of the top node), ρva that of the air, rh = rv the aerodynamic resistances to heat and vapor transfer, and rs the soil surface resistance to evaporation.

The aerodynamic resistance follows from the wind speed with stability corrections for unstable and stable atmospheric conditions (Monin–Obukhov similarity, iterated) Campbell and Norman (1998); Saito et al. (2006); with no wind, heat is transferred by diffusion only. The soil surface resistance increases as the surface dries van de Griend and Owe (1994):

rs = 10 exp[35.63 (0.15 − θ)] for θ < 0.15; rs = 10 s/m otherwise

Coupling

The energy balance is solved together with the heat transport for the surface temperature; the evaporation becomes the flux condition of the water flow, limited by hCritA (atmospheric boundary condition). With vapor flow, the latent heat carried through the surface by the soil vapor flux is returned to the top node. The time step is limited so that the surface temperature changes smoothly.
