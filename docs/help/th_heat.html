Theory and equations

Heat transport

Neglecting the effects of water vapor diffusion, one-dimensional heat transport is described by Sophocleous (1979); Šimůnek et al. (2009):

Cp(θ) ∂T/∂t = ∂/∂x [ λ(θ) ∂T/∂x ] − Cw q ∂T/∂x − Cw S T
SymbolMeaning
Ttemperature [K or °C]
Cp, Cwvolumetric heat capacities of the porous medium and of the liquid phase [M L⁻¹ T⁻² K⁻¹]
λ(θ)apparent thermal conductivity of the soil [M L T⁻³ K⁻¹]
qwater flux [L/T]
Ssink term of root water uptake

The first term on the right is conduction, the second convection by flowing water, the third the heat removed with the root water uptake. With vapor flow the equation contains the latent heat of the vapor flux as well Saito et al. (2006).

Heat capacity

Following de Vries (1963):

Cp(θ) = Cn θn + Co θo + Cw θ + Cv av

with the volume fractions θn (solid phase), θo (organic matter), θ (water) and av (air), and the corresponding volumetric heat capacities (Cn = 1.92, Co = 2.51, Cw = 4.18 MJ/m³/K; the air term is small).

Thermal conductivity

The apparent thermal conductivity combines the thermal conductivity of the soil without flow, λ0(θ), with the macrodispersivity, a linear function of the water flux de Marsily (1986):

λ(θ) = λ0(θ) + βt Cw |q|

with the longitudinal thermal dispersivity βt (λL). λ0 follows Chung and Horton (1987):

λ0(θ) = b1 + b2 θ + b3 θ0.5

with the empirical coefficients b1, b2, b3; for sand 0.228, −2.406, 4.909, for loam 0.243, 0.393, 1.534 and for clay −0.197, −0.962, 2.521 W/m/K. Alternatively the function of Campbell (1985) computes λ0 from the volume fractions of quartz, other minerals and clay.

Boundary conditions

At the surface and the bottom, the temperature (first type) or the heat flux (third type) is prescribed; the bottom can also have a zero gradient. The heat flux condition carries the heat of the water crossing the boundary:

−λ ∂T/∂x + Cw q T = Cw q T0

A sinusoidal daily surface temperature T(t) = T̄ + A sin(2π t/p − 7π/12) can be prescribed (maximum at 13:00), or the surface temperature follows from the surface energy balance.

Numerical solution

The heat transport equation is solved with Galerkin finite elements and the Crank–Nicolson scheme, like the solute transport, after the water flow of each time step.
